# DAY 18 — Research Notes and Claim Boundaries

Research checked on **25 October 2026**. These references support general textile-care guidance; they do not prove how every hijab fabric will behave.

## Sources
1. University of Georgia Cooperative Extension, **Understand Your Fibers**  
   https://site.extension.uga.edu/textiles/textile-basics/understand-your-fibers/  
   Useful boundary: fiber families differ in wrinkle resistance and heat sensitivity; care instructions vary.

2. New Mexico State University, **Pressing Pointers**  
   https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_c/C309/index.html  
   Useful boundary: pressing uses heat, moisture, and pressure; the appropriate amounts depend on the fabric, and excess can flatten or dull a surface.

3. University of Florida IFAS Extension, **Ironing versus Pressing**  
   https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/4H415  
   Useful boundary: start from fabric requirements; a damp press cloth can provide moisture when direct steam is unsuitable.

4. SUNY Oneonta, **Quick & Easy Stain Removal Tips**  
   https://suny.oneonta.edu/why-oneonta/student-life/campus-living/comforts-home/quick-easy-stain-removal-tips  
   Useful boundary: overloading, overdrying, delayed removal, and storage while crumpled can contribute to wrinkles; recommendations remain fabric-dependent.

5. Pratt Institute Textile Research Library, **Cellulosic Fibers**  
   https://textileresearchlab.pratt.edu/fibers/cellulosic-fibers  
   Useful boundary: moisture can alter hydrogen bonding in cellulosic fibers and contribute to shape change; this mechanism should not be generalized to every blend or finish.

## Editorial decisions
- Avoid “voal always wrinkles” or “polyester never wrinkles.” Fiber name alone is insufficient because weave, blend, weight, and finishing vary.
- Avoid promising that steam will fix every wrinkle.
- Always tell the reader to follow the care label, use the lowest effective heat, and test a small area.
- No commercial product recommendation, affiliate cue, or fabric-quality judgment.
