# DAY 31 — Research Notes

**Checked:** 9 November 2026  
**Scope:** eyeglass parts, fit, and safety boundary. The styling advice itself is an editorial hypothesis, not a clinical recommendation.

## Facts used

- The American Academy of Ophthalmology describes temples as the long arms on the side of a frame that fit over the ears.
- American Optometric Association training material treats how eyewear feels at the nose, ears, and temples as part of troubleshooting frame fit.
- AAO guidance notes that frame fit or material may cause irritation and that an optical shop may make minor adjustments; persistent symptoms warrant eye-care follow-up.

## Editorial observations / hypotheses

- A flatter fabric layer, reduced temple bulk, and a directed side drape are visual styling variables that can be compared.
- A 30-second “wear · turn · speak” check is a content workflow proposed for observation; it is not an evidence-backed medical test.
- The package does not recommend bending, heating, or self-adjusting prescription frames.

## Release boundary

If the audience reports persistent pressure, redness, pain, headache, or vision issues, the content directs them to an optician, optometrist, or eye-care professional rather than offering a universal styling fix.
