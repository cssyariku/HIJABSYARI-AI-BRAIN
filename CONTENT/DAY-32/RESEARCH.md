## Supported principles used

- Textiles should be clean and dry before storage.
- Flat storage with minimal folding reduces stress for many textiles.
- When folding is unavoidable, folds should be padded/softened and repositioned periodically rather than repeatedly stressing one line.
- Rolled storage can suit some flat textiles, but it requires support and is not appropriate for every construction or condition.
- Storage should reduce exposure to dust, light, humidity fluctuations, and unnecessary handling or pressure.

## Adaptation boundary

These sources are conservation guidance, often written for collections rather than everyday clothing. The carousel uses only broad preventive principles that translate reasonably to household hijab storage. It does not require museum-grade materials, does not prescribe archival tissue for routine wardrobe use, and does not claim that one storage method prevents wrinkles or deterioration in every fabric.

## Fact / observation / hypothesis

- **Fact from sources:** clean/dry storage, fewer folds, reduced pressure, and suitable storage environment are standard preventive textile-care principles.
- **Editorial observation:** broad loose folds and easy-access organization are visually understandable for a daily hijab wardrobe.
